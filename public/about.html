The method

From daily observations to a planning signal.

For a chosen location, month, and day window, the app fetches daily archive values and counts how often each condition was met. It is a frequency statement about the past — not a deterministic claim about a future date.

  1. 01

    Fetch the archive

    Daily maximum, minimum, and mean temperature, precipitation sum, and maximum wind speed are requested from the Open-Meteo Archive API for the selected year range.

  2. 02

    Build the window

    A selected day uses a ±2-day window to improve sample size. A full-month selection uses every day in that month. Only valid numeric observations are counted.

  3. 03

    Apply explicit thresholds

    Heat ≥ 32 °C · Cold ≤ 5 °C · Rain ≥ 1 mm · Wind ≥ 35 km/h. Thresholds are visible and editable, so the definition of a “risky” day is never hidden.

  4. 04

    Count qualifying days

    Probability = qualifying observations ÷ total valid observations × 100. The interface always shows the count, the sample size, and coverage alongside the percentage.

Sparse locations or periods fall back to “Insufficient data” rather than guessing.

The five risk dimensions

The risk rows translate raw archive values into the conditions that most often ruin an outdoor event. Each one is a simple, inspectable rule.

  • Very Hot

    Days whose maximum temperature reaches or exceeds the heat threshold.

  • Very Cold

    Days whose minimum temperature drops to or below the cold threshold.

  • Very Windy

    Days whose maximum wind speed reaches or exceeds the wind threshold.

  • Very Wet

    Days whose total precipitation reaches or exceeds the rain threshold.

  • Very Uncomfortable

    The inverse of the transparent comfort score, combining the four risks above into a single planning indicator. It is not an official meteorological index.

Data sources, kept distinct

The app deliberately separates observations, short-term forecasts, and imagery. Hover labels and section notes state which is which.

Weather Parade data sources and their roles
Source Role Type
Open-Meteo Archive Historical daily values used for probability calculations Observation
Open-Meteo Forecast Current conditions and a short-term outlook Forecast
OpenStreetMap / Nominatim Location search, reverse geocoding, and base map tiles Reference
NASA GIBS MODIS Terra corrected reflectance imagery for the selected date Imagery
NASA Worldview · Giovanni · GES DISC · Earthdata Search Official research and dataset discovery workflows Research links

What this app does not claim

  • No long-range forecast. Probabilities describe past frequency, not a prediction for a specific future date.

  • No fabricated NASA data. GIBS imagery loads from NASA’s public service. Direct GES DISC or Earthdata downloads may require authentication and are linked, not simulated.

  • No official warning role. For safety-critical decisions, always consult the relevant national meteorological service.

  • No hidden assumptions. Thresholds, windows, sample counts, and coverage are visible in the interface and included in exports.
